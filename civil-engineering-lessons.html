What Civil Engineering Is Teaching Me

Lessons I'm discovering beyond textbooks, equations and classroom assignments.

Newton Wanyoike@asterisk58
A field note from the journey

IN THIS ARTICLE Looking beyondEvery decisionPatience & mistakesBeyond the classroomThe beginning

Learning to see the systems behind the structures.

When I think about civil engineering, I think about buildings, roads, bridges, and the structures that shape the world around us. But the more I learn, the more I realise that this field is about much more than putting physical structures together.

It's also teaching me how to think, how to approach problems, and how to connect what I learn in class to the real world. And honestly, that's becoming one of the most interesting parts of this journey.

01 Learning to look beyond what I see

Before, it was easy for me to look at a beautiful building and admire the finished structure without thinking much about what it took to create it. But as I progress in my studies, I find myself getting more curious about what lies beneath the surface. What supports the structure? Why was a particular material chosen? How are loads transferred through the different structural elements? What calculations helped determine the dimensions?

A structure that looks simple from the outside can represent months of planning, calculations, coordination, and decision-making. I'm beginning to appreciate that engineering involves more than designing what people can see. It also means understanding the systems, forces, and details that make a structure work as intended.

“The finished structure is only the visible part of the thinking that made it possible.”

02 Understanding that every decision matters

One thing I'm learning is that even small design decisions can have significant consequences. Changing a beam's dimensions can affect its structural behaviour, while a change in drainage design can influence the surrounding environment. It reminds me that we can't focus on individual details without considering the bigger picture.

Civil engineers have to think beyond ideal conditions, too. Designs need to account for things like severe weather, expected loads, material deterioration, and other conditions that could affect a structure over time. Calculations need to be accurate, drawings need to communicate the right information, and materials need to be suitable for their intended use.

All these decisions contribute to the safety, durability, and sustainability of the structures and systems we build. I'm starting to understand why engineering requires so much care and attention to detail. What looks like a small decision on paper can have much bigger consequences in the real world.

03 Patience, discipline, and the value of mistakes

Not every concept makes sense immediately. Some calculations take several attempts, and some topics require me to revisit the basics before I can understand more advanced ideas. That's part of learning, even when it can be frustrating.

I'm learning that understanding something properly matters more than pretending to understand it quickly. Getting a calculation wrong can reveal a gap in my understanding, while a difficult assignment can show me which areas need more work.

Of course, making mistakes isn't the goal. The important thing is what I do afterwards: figuring out what went wrong, correcting my understanding, and trying again. Sometimes, progress comes from getting the right answer. Other times, it comes from finally understanding why my first answer was wrong.

04 Engineering is bigger than the classroom

The classroom gives me the foundation, but real engineering is about connecting that knowledge to people's needs and everyday lives.

A road affects transportation, trade, and access to essential services. A drainage system can influence public health, protect the environment, and help reduce flooding in communities. A building is more than concrete, steel, and technical drawings. It's a place where people live, work, learn, and create memories.

Thinking about these connections makes me appreciate the bigger purpose of what I'm studying. Engineering isn't just about making structures stand or ensuring that systems function. It's also about understanding who will use them, how these systems affect their surroundings, and what responsibilities come with designing them.

05 Becoming an engineer, one lesson at a time

I'm still a student, and there's a lot I haven't learnt yet, let alone experienced. Becoming a competent engineer will take more than passing exams. It'll require practical experience, mentorship, continuous learning, and the willingness to keep improving long after my formal education.

But I don't think the journey begins on graduation day. It begins now, in the questions I ask, the concepts I work to understand, the skills I practise, and the habits I develop. Every assignment, every challenge, and every new thing I learn is an opportunity to build a stronger foundation for the engineer I hope to become.

I'm not just studying how to build structures. I'm learning how to approach problems, think critically, and see the world through an engineer's eyes. I've still got a long way to go, but I'm beginning to understand what this journey means to me.

AND THIS IS ONLY THE BEGINNING.
